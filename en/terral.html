Terral · Elqui Valley Observatory

Can observations from weather stations in the valley help us predict when the terral wind will start? We explore that question with an app that brings weather data and machine learning estimates together.

View the observatory ↗ Source code on GitHub ↗

The Problem

The terral is a warm, dry wind characteristic of Chile’s Elqui Valley. The project identifies conditions associated with the wind and estimates whether they may develop in the next few hours. It uses weather criteria as a proxy for the phenomenon, so individual episodes remain unverified.

Data and Application

The system collects and validates weather data, stores it in SQLite, and uses logistic regression models to generate estimates for the web dashboard. Python handles the data and models, and JavaScript supports the interface.

  1. Collection. CEAZA publishes records from the INIA Vicuña and Pisco Elqui weather stations through CEAZAMet.
  2. Preparation. The pipeline validates observations and builds time windows with data from both stations.
  3. Estimation. The models use temperature, humidity, wind, and pressure to estimate when the weather criteria might first be met.
  4. Dashboard. The interface shows estimates by forecast horizon and charts of observations over time.

Evaluation

We trained six L2-regularized logistic regression models for horizons of 1, 3, 6, 8, 12, and 24 hours. We used data from 2013 through 2021 for training and data from 2022 and 2023 for evaluation.

We compare the estimates against a fixed-probability baseline. We also measure which episodes the models predict, how much lead time they provide, and how often they raise false alarms. The observatory’s methodology documents the results and evaluation conditions.

Scope and Next Steps

The observatory is experimental. A six-hour horizon represents a window in which the wind could start. The estimates also depend on which observations are available, how recent they are, and how the system interprets their timestamps.

The next steps are to extend evaluation to 2024 through 2026 and study the trade-off between lead time, detection, and false alarms.
